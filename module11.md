## PART 1: COMMUNICATION TOOLS

Remote practices run on written communication. If you master these four, you can work for 90% of remote employers.

### 1. Email — Your Official Record

Email is still the official channel for patient communication, referrals, prior authorizations, and inter-provider messages.

**What employers expect:**
- Inbox Zero mindset: every email is replied, filed, tasked, or escalated within SLA (often 4-24 hours)
- Clear subject lines: `[Action Required] Referral — J. Smith, DOB 04/12/1978`, `[Confirmed] Telehealth 14 May 2pm ET — Dr. Patel`
- Professional signature with name, title, practice, phone, hours + time zone (EAT + UTC+3)
- Folders/labels: Inbox, Waiting On Doctor, Waiting On Patient, Waiting On Insurance, Completed, Urgent

**Kenya context:** Many Kenyan professionals check email once a day. Remote employers expect 2-4 inbox checks per day minimum, with Slack/Teams for urgent items. Set Gmail/Outlook notifications on your phone + laptop during work hours.

**Template — Professional Patient Email:**

> Subject: Appointment Confirmation — [Practice Name] — 14 May at 2:00 PM ET
>
> Dear Ms. Johnson,
>
> This is confirming your telehealth appointment with Dr. Patel on Wednesday, 14 May at 2:00 PM Eastern Time (9:00 PM East Africa Time).
>
> Join link: [Zoom link]
> Please join 10 minutes early and have your ID and medication list ready.
>
> Reply YES to confirm or let us know if you need to reschedule.
>
> Warm regards,
> [Your Full Name]
> Medical Virtual Assistant | [Practice Name]
> [Phone] | Working Hours: 3pm–11pm EAT (8am–4pm ET)

**Common email tools:** Gmail (Google Workspace), Outlook (Microsoft 365). Learn both — US small practices often use Gmail; UK/NHS-linked and larger US groups often use Outlook/Teams.

### 2. Microsoft Teams — The Hospital Corridor, Online

Teams is dominant in larger practices, UK, and Australian clients because it bundles chat, video, files (SharePoint/OneDrive), and calendar.

**Must-learn skills:**
- Chat vs Channel vs Thread: Use Channels (e.g., #scheduling, #referrals, #billing) not DMs for work others need to see
- Status: Set Available / Busy / In Meeting accurately; add status message: `Working EAT 3pm-11pm | Lunch 6-6:30pm`
- File collaboration: Co-edit a referral tracker in real time; @mention the doctor only when decision needed
- Meeting etiquette: Mute on entry, camera on for patient/internal meetings unless told otherwise, blur background

**Practical workflow:** Morning huddle in Teams → Check Planner/Tasks → Update channel: `Morning update: 12 appointments confirmed, 2 pending insurance, 1 urgent refill flagged for Dr. Lee`

### 3. Zoom — Where Telehealth Happens

Zoom is used for team meetings AND patient telehealth visits (often via HIPAA-enabled Zoom for Healthcare, Doxy.me, or EHR-integrated video).

**As a VA, you will:**
- Schedule Zoom visits with correct time zone, waiting room + passcode enabled
- Send pre-visit tech checks: camera/mic test, quiet room, ID ready
- Admit patients from waiting room, troubleshoot: "Can you hear me? Please tap Join Audio"
- Take admin notes, manage chat, share screen for intake forms
- Record ONLY if employer authorizes and patient consents — never record privately

**Kenya + US/UK/Australia context:** Your internet must sustain 10+ Mbps up/down. Test at fast.com before shift. Have backup: Safaricom/Airtel hotspot + charged power bank. If video freezes, immediately switch to phone audio + chat so the patient is never left alone.

**Pre-visit checklist (copy-paste to patient):**
- [ ] Join 10 mins early via link
- [ ] Quiet, private room, good lighting on your face
- [ ] ID + medication list + insurance card (US) / NHS number (UK) / Medicare (Australia) ready
- [ ] Headphones recommended

### 4. Slack — Fast, Informal Team Coordination

US startups, billing companies, and VA agencies love Slack.

**Must-learn skills:**
- Channels: #general, #patient-ops, #urgent, #wins. Keep #urgent ONLY for truly urgent (clinical concern, same-day cancellation)
- Threads: Always reply in thread to avoid chaos
- Huddles: Quick audio huddle instead of 10 messages
- Slash commands: `/remind @team Follow up prior auth #1234 tomorrow 9am`
- Integrations: Google Calendar, Asana/Trello reminders appear in Slack

**Professional Slack example:**
> @dr-patel Flagging: Patient M.K. (DOB 02/03/1965) requesting urgent refill — Lisinopril. Last visit 2 months ago. No adverse symptoms reported. Awaiting your approval. Chart updated. — Faith, 15:22 EAT

**Table: When to use what**

| Situation | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Official patient confirmation, referral letter | Email | Auditable record |
| Quick internal question, daily updates | Teams / Slack | Speed + visibility |
| Patient visit, team meeting, interview | Zoom / Teams Meeting | Face + trust |
| Urgent clinical concern | Phone + Teams/Slack #urgent + EHR flag | Never email-only for urgent |

## PART 2: PRODUCTIVITY TOOLS

### 5. Google Workspace (Gmail, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Calendar)

Most US small practices and VA agencies run on Google.

**Skills to master:**
- **Gmail:** Labels, filters (auto-label `Referral` emails), templates/canned responses, snooze, schedule-send to match US morning
- **Drive:** Folder structure: `01_Patients_Admin / 02_Referrals / 03_Insurance / 04_Reports`. Never store PHI on personal Drive — only employer-provided Drive.
- **Docs/Sheets:** Track referrals, daily reports. Learn `=TODAY()`, filters, conditional formatting (red = overdue), comments + suggest mode
- **Calendar:** Create events with Zoom link, correct time zone, reminders (24h + 1h), color-code: Blue=confirmed, Yellow=pending, Red=cancelled

### 6. Microsoft 365 (Outlook, OneDrive, Word, Excel, Teams Calendar)

UK/Australia and hospital-affiliated US practices often use Microsoft 365.

**Skills to master:**
- Outlook rules, focused inbox, shared mailboxes (`info@clinic.com`)
- Excel basics: sort/filter, freeze panes, data validation, XLOOKUP/VLOOKUP for reconciling lists
- OneDrive/SharePoint version control — never email `Referral_Tracker_FINAL_v7.docx` around

**Tip for Kenyans:** Practice both ecosystems. In interviews say: "I work daily in Google Workspace and Microsoft 365, including shared calendars across EAT/ET/GMT."

### 7. Calendar Systems — Your Most Important Clinical Safety Tool

A calendar mistake = missed patient = patient safety + revenue issue.

**Rules:**
1. Always create events with BOTH zones in title when cross-border: `Telehealth — A. Brown — 2pm ET / 9pm EAT`
2. Enable working hours + secondary time zone in Google/Outlook (Nairobi + New York/London/Sydney)
3. Buffer time: 10 mins between telehealth visits for notes
4. Confirm 24-48h ahead + same-day reminder

**Template — Calendar Invite Description:**
```
Patient: A. Brown | DOB: 11/08/1980 | Phone: +1...
Provider: Dr. Patel | Type: Follow-up Telehealth (20 min)
Link: [Zoom]
Pre-visit: Insurance verified | Intake form received | Labs attached
```

### 8. Task-Management Platforms (Trello, Asana, ClickUp, Monday, Notion)

Remote teams track work in boards, not memory.

**Example Trello board: Patient Ops**
- Lists: To Do → Doing → Waiting (Doctor/Patient/Insurance) → Done
- Card: `Prior Auth — J. Smith — Aetna — #PA-8841` with due date, checklist, attachment, @assignee
- Daily ritual: Move cards, comment progress, tag blockers

**Workflow for Kenyan VAs:** Start of shift (15 mins): Review board → Prioritize by due + urgency → Post plan in Slack. End of shift (15 mins): Update all cards + end-of-day report.

## PART 3: HEALTHCARE PLATFORMS

You will not be asked to diagnose. You WILL be asked to find, update, and protect information accurately.

### 9. EMR/EHR Systems (e.g., Epic, Cerner/Oracle Health, Athenahealth, eClinicalWorks, SimplePractice, Kareo)

**Core admin skills (transferable across all EHRs):**
- Search patient by MRN/DOB (never by name alone — verify two identifiers)
- View schedule, check-in, confirm insurance/consent flags
- Upload documents to correct chart section, correct date
- Add admin notes: factual, timestamped, no clinical opinions: `15 May 9:20pm EAT: Patient called to reschedule 16 May visit to 20 May due to transport. Offered 20 May 2pm ET, patient accepted. Reminder sent. — F.N.`
- Task routing: Send refill/referral task to provider pool; never leave in your inbox

**Golden rule:** If you didn't document it, it didn't happen. If you documented an opinion instead of a fact, you created risk.

**Kenya context:** If you used DHIS2, KenyaEMR, or hospital HMIS in Kenya, say so: "Experienced in KenyaEMR/DHIS2 — fast learner on Epic/Athenahealth, strong on accurate data entry and privacy."

### 10. Practice Management Platforms (scheduling, billing, eligibility)

Examples: AthenaOne, AdvancedMD, DrChrono, Jane (UK/Aus allied health). They handle scheduling, reminders, eligibility checks, claims status.

**Your tasks:** Verify demographics + insurance, send intake forms, check eligibility (US: active coverage? copay? referral needed?), track claim status: Submitted → Acknowledged → Paid / Denied → Action.

### 11. Patient Portals (MyChart, FollowMyHealth, etc.)

Patients message, request refills, view labs via portals. You triage messages: admin (address change, appointment) = handle; clinical (new chest pain, medication side effect) = route urgently to clinician per SOP — never advise clinically.

**Script:**
> "Thank you for messaging us. I've flagged your symptoms to Dr. Patel's clinical team as urgent. If this worsens or you have chest pain/breathing difficulty, please call [emergency number: 911 US / 999 UK / 000 Australia] or go to urgent care immediately. We'll update you within [X hours]."

### 12. Telehealth Platforms (Zoom for Healthcare, Doxy.me, Amwell, Teladoc, EHR video)

Workflow: Schedule → Verify consent + ID → Send link + prep → Room/test → Visit support → Post-visit: summary, referrals, follow-up booked, patient survey.

## PART 4: AI — RESPONSIBLE USE FOR PRODUCTIVITY

AI (ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, EHR ambient tools) is now expected — but privacy mistakes with AI can end your contract instantly.

### What you MAY use AI for (with non-sensitive info only):

1. **Drafting administrative communication:** "Draft a polite reschedule email for a patient who missed telehealth due to tech issues" → then human-edit for tone + accuracy
2. **Summarizing non-sensitive information:** Summarize a public insurance article, not a patient chart
3. **Transcription assistance:** ONLY on employer-authorized, HIPAA-compliant tools (e.g., EHR dictation). Never paste patient audio into public AI.
4. **Task organization:** "Turn this to-do list into a prioritized EOD checklist"
5. **Research assistance:** "Explain US prior authorization steps for Aetna in plain English" → verify on official payer site
6. **Productivity:** Generate SOP checklist, Excel formulas, polite follow-up variants

### What you must NEVER do:

- Never paste names, DOB, MRN, photos, IDs, diagnoses, prescriptions into public ChatGPT/Gemini/Copilot
- Never upload patient documents to free summarizers/transcribers
- Never let AI send messages to patients without human review
- Never trust AI for clinical advice, dosage, or triage — escalate to clinician

**Responsible AI workflow:**
1. De-identify → 2. Draft with AI → 3. Verify facts + tone → 4. Get approval if SOP requires → 5. Send via official channel → 6. Document

**Good vs Bad prompt:**
- Bad: "Summarize this patient note: Jane Smith DOB... has HIV..." ❌
- Good: "Draft a generic template for requesting missing insurance information from a US patient, polite and HIPAA-aware, no PHI included" ✅

> **Privacy reminder:** Sensitive patient information should not be entered into AI tools unless the employer has explicitly authorized the tool in writing and appropriate privacy protections (e.g., BAA, HIPAA-compliant instance) are in place. When in doubt, ask your supervisor. This aligns with Kenya Data Protection Act principles: lawful purpose, data minimization, and safeguards.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Using personal Gmail/Drive/WhatsApp for patient data.** Always use employer-provided systems.
2. **Time-zone errors.** Double-bookings destroy trust. Always include both zones and use calendar zone features.
3. **Pasting PHI into public AI.** Instant dismissal + legal risk. De-identify and use only authorized tools.
4. **Leaving patients in Zoom waiting rooms.** Monitor waiting room + chat; message if doctor delayed.
5. **Chat chaos:** Posting patient details in wrong Slack channel, or DMing what should be visible to team.
6. **No documentation:** Doing work but not updating EHR/task board — team assumes it wasn't done.
7. **Poor audio/background:** Taking patient calls in noisy cyber café or with unprofessional background.
8. **Ignoring notifications after shift without handover.** Always hand over pending items in writing.
