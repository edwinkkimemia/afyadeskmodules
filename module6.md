## 1. How Telemedicine Works: Flow, Models, and Your Invisible Value

End-to-end flow you own: online booking → automated intake + insurance check → VA verification (eligibility, forms, consent, ID) → secure link + prep checklist sent → clinician video visit (you monitor waiting room) → e-prescription + lab/referral orders launched → summary sent in 2 hours → follow-up booked → billing + recall + satisfaction survey. Miss one handoff and visit fails: no link = no-show, no consent = compliance breach, no summary = confused patient + callback flood.

Four models:

**Synchronous video (core):** Real-time Zoom for Healthcare, Doxy.me, Amwell, EHR video. Best for new, chronic review, mental health (45–50 min). Needs 10+ Mbps, camera/mic, private room both ends, backup phone. US reimburses similar to in-person for many codes post-2020; UK NHS counts video toward access targets; Australia MBS has telehealth items (verify current). Your tasks: link, tech-check, waiting-room monitoring, timekeeping, post-summary.

**Audio-only phone:** US Medicare/Rural + elderly without video, UK phone-first triage. Shorter (10–15 min), needs clear documentation (consent for phone, callback numbers, background noise note). Your tasks: call bridge, read-back, SMS summary (phone has no visual cues — over-confirm).

**Asynchronous store-and-forward:** Derm photos, e-consults, questionnaires reviewed later. Needs image quality checklist (good light, ruler for size, 3 angles), consent for photos, secure upload (never personal WhatsApp), turnaround SLA (24–48h). Your tasks: request quality images, chase missing, route to clinician, relay plan.

**Remote Patient Monitoring (RPM):** BP cuffs, glucometers, pulse oximeters uploading to portal (e.g., Omron + portal). US CPT 99453–99458 pays for setup + monitoring. Your tasks: enroll, teach device, check uploads weekly, flag gaps (“No readings 5 days — call patient, check battery/WiFi, resend guide”), escalate critical readings per SOP (never interpret alone — “BP 190/110 reported via device — flagged urgent to nurse pool 2:05pm ET, advised ER if symptoms, called patient to confirm seeking care”).

Your value: make tech invisible so doctor focuses on medicine and patient feels cared for, not processed. Practices keep VAs who cut video no-shows from 18% to 8% and handle tech failures without panic.

## 2. Virtual Consultations: T-24h, T-1h, Live, and Post Checklists That Prevent Disaster

**T-24h (day before, 4pm):** Confirm all tomorrow videos via SMS + email with dual-zone + link + prep (ID, meds list, insurance, vitals if RPM, quiet private room, headphones). Verify consent signed (telehealth risks, recording policy — usually no recording without written consent both sides, emergency plan), eligibility active, intake + previous labs attached, chart prepped (PMH, meds, allergies bolded, last note summarized for doctor in 3 lines). Test your own Zoom host + waiting room + chat + screen-share. Flag gaps to doctor: “2/14 missing intake — chased, deadline 8am ET.”

**T-1h (green-room):** Open visit 30 min early, admit early joins to holding message (“Dr. Lee will join at 10:30 AM ET — please stay, test mic/camera, have ID ready”), verify backup phone on file (“If video drops, we’ll call +1-... immediately — is that best?”), confirm patient location + emergency contact for telehealth safety (“Where are you joining from today? Local emergency number?” — required US safety standard), lighting/audio check (“I can see/hear you clearly — doctor will too”). Message doctor: “Room ready, patient waiting, chart + consent complete.”

**Live monitoring (you stay present, muted):** Track waiting room (never leave patient alone >5 min without message), timekeep (“10 min left” private chat to doctor if overrun risk), troubleshoot instantly via chat: “Can’t hear? Tap Join Audio → WiFi → allow mic. Still stuck? I’ll call you now at +1-... while keeping video open.” Document late joins/no-shows with timestamps, offer phone conversion in 5 min (“Video stuck? Let’s switch to phone now so you don’t lose slot — I’ll call +1-... in 1 min, summary by SMS after”). For overruns, message next patient proactively (“Dr. Lee running 10 min late — thank you for patience, still with you, link active”) — prevents abandonment + bad reviews.

**Post-visit (within 2 hours):** Send summary (diagnosis in plain English as documented by clinician — never add interpretation, meds with doses/times, lab/referral next steps with dates, red flags + who to call, follow-up booked dual-zone), e-prescription routing confirmed (“Sent to CVS Main St — pickup in 2 hrs, text when ready”), lab slips + referral packets launched with tracker entries, tasks closed, recall set, satisfaction survey link. Book follow-up before patient forgets: “Follow-up Tue 28 May 10am ET / 5pm EAT booked — reply YES — SMS reminder 48h + 24h.” Log total visit time + tech issues for improvement.

Tech-fail playbook (memorize 4 scripts): frozen video → “Let’s turn off video, stay on audio — I can still hear you clearly — I’ll resend video link via SMS while we continue.” No audio → “I’ll call you now at backup +1-... in 30 seconds — keep this window open.” Link expired → new link in 1 min + SMS + email + phone bridge. Full outage (your fibre cut) → hotspot switch + Slack flag + SMS to waiting patients + phone-only mode. Never go silent — silence reads as abandonment. Post flag in 2 min with impact + workaround + next update time.

## 3. Patient Onboarding for Telehealth: From Anxious to Ready in 10 Minutes

Packet (portal invite + ID upload + insurance front/back + telehealth consent + tech guide PDF + test-call offer + FAQ + “what to expect” 2-min video). For new, call 10-min tech check day before — reduces video no-shows 30% (cite in interviews). Checklist: device (phone/laptop charged), browser (Chrome updated), bandwidth (fast.com >10 Mbps, close Netflix), room (quiet, private, lit face, door closed), ID + meds + insurance at desk, backup phone verified, emergency location noted, consent e-signed.

Elderly/low-digital script: “Mrs. Smith, we’ll do a quick 10-min practice today so tomorrow is easy — no rush. Can you tap the blue link I just texted? ... Perfect — I see you! Can you tap microphone to unmute? Wonderful — tomorrow same link, I’ll be here 10 min early to welcome you. If stuck, just answer my call at +1-... and we’ll switch to phone — you won’t lose your visit.” Patience + praise + fallback removes fear. Offer phone alternative without shame, involve family with consent (“Would daughter help with setup? I can 3-way call now”).

Consent must cover: video vs in-person limits (no physical exam), privacy risks (use private room, encrypted platform), recording policy (no patient recording without permission and vice versa — state clearly), emergency plan (location + local ER + callback if disconnected), data storage (EHR, retention), right to switch to in-person. Document consent date/time/method. For minors, guardian consent + presence rules per state (US varies — follow SOP, don’t assume).

## 4. Telehealth Scheduling Nuances That Prevent Chaos

Buffers: 10 min between videos for notes + overrun + bio break. Never back-to-back new 40-min videos — 50-min mental health needs 10-min decompress + notes or quality collapses. Cluster zones: UK morning block (2–5pm EAT) then US block (5–11pm EAT) to reduce mental switching + keep inbox SLAs per region. Note every booking: video vs phone vs async, link sent? + tested?, consent signed?, backup phone, time zone both, interpreter needed?, RPM device data reviewed?

Durations: new video 30–40, follow-up 15–20, chronic + RPM review 20–30, mental health intake 50 + follow-up 25, results review 15, urgent triage 15 buffer. Ask reason to slot: “New rash with photos ready → derm async + 15-min video review” vs “Chest pain today → ER/urgent, not routine video in 2 weeks.” Double-book rule: never same doctor overlapping even virtual — tech delays cascade double. If urgent add-on needed, offer phone triage slot or extend day with approval, document consent.

Calendar titles: “VIDEO — Smith, J 1980-05-14 — 10:30 AM ET / 5:30 PM EAT — Dr. Lee 20-min FU — Link ... — Backup +1-... — Consent signed.” Description: insurance, intake status, labs attached, language. Colour: green confirmed + tech-checked, yellow pending forms/consent, red tech risk/no backup. Friday audit: next Mon–Tue 100% link-tested, gaps waitlisted.

## 5. Digital Patient Communication: Portal, SMS, Email With Triage Discipline

Approved only: EHR portal messages, practice SMS (Weave, SimpleTexting, Textline), business email via employer domain. Never personal WhatsApp/Gmail for PHI — no BAA, no audit, instant dismissal. Every digital message logged to chart within 4 hours.

Triage 3 buckets hourly: **Urgent now** (chest pain, breathing difficulty, bleeding, suicidal thoughts, baby fever, severe allergy — phone + EHR flag + clinician alert 15 min, advise ER 911/999/000, document time/location/callback), **Today** (scheduling, refills without red flags, forms — reply <4 hrs with action + deadline), **This week** (records, non-urgent admin — reply <24h). After-hours auto-reply: “Received outside 9am–5pm ET — reviewed next business morning. For emergency call 911 / go to ER now.” Never leave urgent overnight without phone escalation + handover.

Templates: invite (“Hi {Name}, video {date time zones} Dr. X {link}. Join 10 min early, ID + meds ready. Backup call {number} if video fails. Reply YES. Help: +1-... — Mercy”), reminder 24h + 2h, tech-check (“Tap link now to test — allow camera/mic — reply OK or CALL for help”), post-visit (“Thanks for seeing Dr. Lee today. Summary + Rx sent to portal/email. Labs: {slip} due {date} at {address}. Follow-up {date zones} booked — reply YES. Questions? Reply here 9–5 ET. ER for worsening — Mercy”), lab chase Day 3/7/14, no-show (“We missed you {time} — okay? Phone conversion now? Rebook 7 days? Reply 1/2”). Personalize name/time/link every send — wrong-link macro is worse than slow manual.

## 6. Follow-Up Workflows That Close Loops (Where Money and Safety Live)

**Labs:** order sent → patient notified with slip + address + hours + prep (fasting?) → Day 3 SMS nudge if not done → Day 7 call + barrier fix (cost? transport? fear? offer telehealth + nearby location) → result arrives → route to doctor STAT if critical (lab calls critical — phone immediately) else pool 24h → relay plan verbatim from clinician (never interpret: “Dr. Lee reviewed — please book 20-min review Tue 10am ET to discuss next steps, continue meds meanwhile unless told otherwise, seek care for X”) → book follow-up in 24h of result → close task. Track: ordered/done/reviewed/relayed/follow-up booked. Open >7 days red in huddle.

**Referrals from telehealth:** same 8-step loop as Module 3 plus video summary attached + patient location noted (licensing — some US states require doctor licensed where patient located at visit time — flag if patient travels, don’t decide). Share screen to book specialist live with patient (“Cardiology has Tue 2pm ET or Thu 10am — which suits? ... Booked Tue, SMS sent, auth # attached”). Retrieve note in 7–14 days, route to PCP, close loop. Report closure % monthly.

**No-show video:** call in 15 min (“We missed video 10:30 — tech issue? I can switch to phone now in 1 min or rebook 7 days — which?”), SMS + email with one-tap rebook link, log reason (tech, forgot, privacy — no quiet room, cost). If tech, offer phone + test-call before next. Target 60% rebooked in 7 days. Weekly reason review — if “no quiet room” high, add prep line “private room + headphones needed.”

**Chronic RPM:** enroll (consent + device paired + teach + test upload), monitor dashboard weekly (no data 5 days → call: battery? WiFi? vacation? resend guide, offer phone check), flag gaps to nurse, escalate critical per thresholds (e.g., BP >180/110, glucose <70 with symptoms → urgent + ER advice + document). Praise adherence (“Great — 6/7 readings this week! Doctor will review Tue”) — adherence rises 20% with VA encouragement. Billable RPM needs 16+ readings/month + 20 min review — your tracking enables billing.

## 7. Remote Healthcare Teams: Huddles, RACI, and Handovers That Prevent Drops

Async standup daily 10 min in Slack/Teams #huddle: Yesterday (numbers: 14 videos supported, 12 summaries sent, 2 tech fails converted to phone, 1 urgent escalated 8 min), Today (12 videos, 5 tech-checks, 3 lab chases), Blockers (Aetna portal down — 2 auths retry 9am ET, Dr. Lee calendar locked — access requested). Tag owners + due times. No standup = duplicated calls + missed urgent.

RACI for telehealth day: VA owns scheduling, links, reminders, waiting room, summaries, trackers, EOD (Responsible). Nurse owns triage decisions + critical RPM review (Accountable for clinical). Doctor owns diagnosis/plan (Accountable). Front-desk (if any) Consulted for in-person overflow. Patient Informed via SMS/portal. When unsure who decides (phone vs video conversion for borderline chest pain?), escalate to nurse/doctor — don’t decide alone, document you asked + time + answer.

Handovers written, never verbal-only: “Handing over 11pm EAT: 2 videos tomorrow need intake (rows 4–5), 1 lab critical pending Dr. review (flagged 9pm ET), 1 referral awaiting auth (Aetna #8841, retry 9am ET), 1 no-show to rebook (voicemail left). All in tracker + EHR. @night-team ping till midnight EAT for urgent.” Night team acknowledges with emoji + name. No ack = call. Gap coverage kills patients — handover is safety, not admin.

## 8. EHR, Portals, and Documentation for Virtual Encounters

Chart prep: verify 2 identifiers (name + DOB), demographics, contacts, emergency + backup, insurance + auth, consent (telehealth + treatment + photo if async), meds reconciliation (“Any changes, stops, new over-the-counter/herbal since last visit? Allergies still penicillin rash?” — document verified today, don’t carry forward blindly), allergies bolded, vitals from home devices labelled patient-reported with date/time/device (“BP 142/88 13 May 8am Omron home — patient-reported”), previous labs/imaging attached, reason + urgency + interpreter noted.

Post-visit close same day: video consent + location + duration attached, visit note filed (clinician signs, you ensure admin sections complete), orders launched (labs/referrals/Rx routing confirmed), tasks assigned with due dates (not left in your inbox), recall set (HbA1c 3 months, BP 2 weeks), summary sent + logged, no-show/task-zero verified. Audit yourself: 0 unsigned admin tasks, 0 unscanned docs >24h, tomorrow 100% prepped by 4pm. If EHR downtime, paper backup + transcribe in 4 hours with “downtime entry” flag + double verification.

## 9. Troubleshooting Playbook (Laminate This)

Fibre drops mid-clinic: hotspot on in 2 min, Slack flag with speed (“Fibre cut 8:12pm EAT, hotspot 12 Mbps, continuing chat/phone, video limited 20 min — update 8:30”), SMS waiting patients (“Video briefly audio-only — staying with you, link active”), defer uploads, log outage + mitigation in time tracker. Power dip: laptop + power bank 5 hrs, router UPS 2 hrs, token loaded, alternate location mapped. Zoom “unable to connect”: switch browser → app → phone dial-in + SMS new link. Patient can’t hear: chat “Tap Join Audio → WiFi → allow mic → raise volume → plug headphones → I’ll call backup now.” Echo: mute all except speaker, headphones on, close duplicate tabs. Never blame patient (“You’re muted!”) — guide: “Tap mic icon bottom-left — now I can hear you clearly, thank you!” Log every fail + fix for SOP improvement.
