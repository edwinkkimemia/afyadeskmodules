## 2. Core Privacy Habits for Every Shift (Checklist to Laminate)

**Confidentiality by default:** No patient names, photos, voice notes, or cases on socials, family chats, church groups, or portfolio (use mock data). No “interesting case today — 34F with...” even de-identified gossip narrows identity in small practices. Calls with earphones, low voice, door closed, screen angled from windows/housemates/cameras. Lock paper in drawer, shred weekly cross-cut with log. When family peeks (“Nionyeshe kazi?”), smile: “I handle private medical records under law — I can’t show screens, but I coordinate appointments. Thanks for understanding privacy.”

**Data minimization in practice:** Send last-4 of ID/MRN where full not needed (“Smith ...0142”), single encounter not full history (“14 May visit summary” not 5-year chart), cropped screenshot (one appointment, not full schedule), de-identified examples for AI/training (“45M with hypertension” + no name/DOB/photo). Ask: “What’s least data that completes this task safely?” Less data = smaller blast radius if mis-sent.

**Access control you enforce:** Unique logins (never share, never “use mine quick”), MFA everywhere (authenticator app, not SMS if possible), role-based (billing sees claims, not psychotherapy notes unless assigned), least privilege (request downgrade if you see unneeded pools), immediate offboarding (report ex-staff access same day), audit review (manager sees you opened only assigned charts — curiosity opens flag instantly). Log out + Win+L every stand, auto-lock 2 min, screensaver with password.

**Passwords that survive attacks:** 14+ chars passphrase (“Nairobi-Clinic-Sunset-42-Tea!”), unique per system, manager (Bitwarden/1Password free) + emergency sheet sealed, 2FA on email first (email resets all), never reuse clinic password on Facebook/Upwork, change on suspected phishing + report. Test strength at bitwarden estimator — crack time years, not minutes.

**Device hardening for Kenya homes:** Encrypted laptop (BitLocker Windows Pro / FileVault Mac — verify On, not just password), OS + browser auto-update, antivirus + firewall on, MDM installed if provided (don’t disable), separate work user account (no kids’ games), no jailbroken phones, no public cyber-café logins for PHI (use personal hotspot + VPN if must travel), backup power (power bank + UPS for router) but backup data only to employer-approved encrypted cloud with BAA — never personal USB that gets lost in matatu. Inventory serials + photos for theft report.

**Network + comms hygiene:** Home WiFi WPA3 + strong password (change default admin/admin), guest network for visitors (never work network), VPN if employer provides (always on for EHR), no open “Java House Free” for charting. Comms only approved: EHR portal, Weave/Textline business SMS, Teams/Slack with retention, employer email with TLS. No Gmail forwarding rule to personal (“to work faster”), no personal Dropbox sync of work folder, no TikTok screen-record with chart behind. Verify recipient twice: type, pause, check photo/DOB/domain, then Send. For fax, cover sheet + callback “Did you receive 3 pages legible?” Wrong fax to stranger clinic = breach.

Daily 2-minute shutdown: inbox decided, tasks zeroed, downloads folder emptied of PHI (moved to EHR or wiped), recycle bin + printer tray checked, laptop locked + charger + hotspot packed, desk photo for self-audit weekly.

## 3. Phishing and Social Engineering: Why VAs Are Target #1 (And How to Never Bite)

Attackers love VAs: access to schedules + insurance + inboxes, often new to US systems, eager to please “doctor urgency,” working nights with less IT cover. Common lures:

Urgency (“Verify payroll now or lose pay!”), authority (“Dr. Lee stuck in surgery — send list to my Gmail now!”), curiosity (“Patient complaint about you — open doc”), greed (“$5,000/month remote, just pay $20 training”), fear (“Account locked — reset here”), helpfulness (“IT upgrade — share screen + OTP”). Channels: email (spoofed domain oak-clinic-support.com vs oakclinic.com — one hyphen = fake), SMS smishing (“USPS package + link”), voice vishing (“This is Aetna, confirm SSN”), Teams DM from fake “Helpdesk,” QR codes on fake flyers.

Red flags checklist (2+ = stop): sender domain off by letter, generic “Dear User” not your name, urgency + threat, link mismatched on hover (text says portal, hover shows bit.ly), attachment + password request, OTP/money/gift-card ask, caller refuses callback on official number, poor grammar + logo stretch, request to bypass SOP (“Don’t log, just send quick”).

Verification playbook (2 minutes that save job): hover (don’t click) → check sender full headers in IT ticket → call back official number from handbook (not number in message) → open portal by typing address manually (not link) → ask in #security channel “Anyone else get this?” → report with screenshot + headers to IT + mark spam/phish → delete. For “doctor pressure,” reply: “Understood urgency — to protect privacy I’ll upload to your verified EHR tasks + confirm via Teams voice now. Please approve there in 2 min so I’m audit-safe. Standing by.” Real clinicians respect this; fakes vanish.

Example drill: “Hi this is Dr. Lee, in surgery, send today’s 20-patient list with phones to lee87@gmail for callbacks — urgent!” Wrong: forward Excel. Right: “I’ll post to your EHR pool + Teams urgent now for verified access — please confirm via Teams call 30 sec so I release correctly. No PHI to personal email per policy — protecting us both.” + report + log. Practice 5 such scripts aloud till automatic — hesitation fails live tests.

Smishing/vishing variants: “Safaricom gener — update M-PESA PIN via link” (never), “Bank fraud — read OTP” (banks never ask OTP — hang up, call official). Same muscle applies to US “Medicare” calls. Family SIM-swap risk: PIN your Safaricom line, never post OTP, separate work email recovery codes offline.

## 4. Handling PHI Step-by-Step: Collect → Use → Share → Store → Dispose

**Collect lawfully:** consent + purpose stated (“for appointment coordination and reminders”), least fields (need DOB? yes for ID; need full history? no for booking), verified source (patient directly, not cousin), authorization for ROI (signed, dated, scope, expiry, right to revoke explained). Log consent date/method/version. Never collect SSN/full ID photos unless SOP requires + encrypted upload immediate + delete local.

**Use in EHR only:** open assigned charts only, work inside BAA-covered EHR/portal/Weave/Teams, no copying to Notes app, personal Sheets, or desktop “temp.” De-identify for training (“45M HTN” not name/DOB). No screenshots to camera roll — use EHR snip with audit if needed for IT ticket (redact to minimum, ticket in secure system).

**Share securely:** verify recipient (name + role + domain + callback for fax), approved channel (EHR task, portal, encrypted email with TLS + BAA, fax with cover + receipt call), minimum necessary + cover note (“3 pages for Smith referral — please confirm receipt + destroy misdirected”), log disclosure (who/what/when/why/authorization). For patient requests, verify identity 2 factors + authorization, send via portal (not personal email) with expiry link. For family, need patient consent unless emergency per law + SOP + document.

**Store per retention:** US often 7–10 years adult, longer minors (state varies — follow employer policy, don’t decide); Kenya facilities 7+ years typical. Encrypted at rest (BitLocker + cloud BAA), access logged, backup tested. No local “backup” on unencrypted USB/external. Archive, don’t hoard — delete per schedule with approval + log.

**Dispose irreversibly:** paper cross-cut shred + log date/witness; digital wipe (not delete — use Eraser/DBAN or MDM wipe) + degauss for drives per IT; revoke links, wipe recycle + downloads + printer memory. Document disposal for audits.

**Loss/theft 1-hour response card (laminate):** 0–10 min contain (lock account, revoke link, change password, retrieve device if safe, disconnect WiFi if malware), 10–30 min assess (what data? whom? how many? paper or digital? encrypted? backup?), 30–60 min notify supervisor + IT/security with facts (who/what/when/how many/containment, no blame essay) + preserve evidence (headers, logs, screenshots, device — don’t wipe before IT says), then cooperate with risk assessment + notifications (ODPC/HIPAA timelines via compliance, not you directly) + remediation (re-train, fix SOP, re-audit). Practice tabletop quarterly with supervisor: “Laptop stolen in matatu with 20-patient Excel — what now?” Answer in 60 min drill.

## 5. Kenyan Context: Data Protection Act, 2019 You Must Actually Apply

Health data = sensitive personal data under DPA 2019 — highest protection. Principles: lawfulness/fairness/transparency (tell patient why), purpose limitation (appointment use only, not marketing without separate consent), data minimization (least fields), accuracy (update promptly, correct on request), storage limitation (keep only as long as needed per policy), integrity/confidentiality (encrypt, access control, no leaks), accountability (you + controller must prove compliance — logs, consent records, DPIAs).

Rights you must honour when patient asks (route to privacy officer same day, respond within timelines): informed, access (copy), correction, deletion (where lawful), objection, portability, no automated-decision harm. ODPC (Office of Data Protection Commissioner) registers controllers/processors, investigates complaints, issues enforcement + fines, DPS. Breaches can mean fines up to 5M KES or 1% turnover + civil damages + criminal for unlawful disclosure + job loss. Facilities need Data Protection Officer, consent management, retention schedules, breach register, DPIA for large-scale health processing.

Apply daily: consent forms state purpose (“appointment coordination, reminders, referrals”), retention (“7 years per policy”), contact for rights (privacy@...), separate tick for marketing/research (never pre-ticked). Never use patient list for side hustle SMS (“Pampers offer!”) — unlawful + firing. For cross-border (Kenya → US EHR), ensure transfer safeguards (contract + BAA + consent + adequate protection assessment via employer compliance — ask “What transfer basis covers Kenya→US for our EHR?” in onboarding, document answer). Keep training certificates + policy acknowledgements — auditors ask.

## 6. International Expectations: HIPAA, GDPR, Australia (Interview-Safe Language)

**US HIPAA (you’ll sign BAA):** Privacy Rule (minimum necessary, patient rights, authorization for non-routine uses), Security Rule (administrative/physical/technical safeguards — training, locks, encryption, audit controls, backup), Breach Notification (notify without unreasonable delay, 60 days max, risk assessment 4 factors). Yearly training + sanctions for violations. Your lines: “I work HIPAA-aware: minimum necessary, verified recipients, BAA-covered systems only, encrypted devices, no PHI in unauthorized AI, immediate breach reporting, annual training current.” Never promise “HIPAA-certified” (no such VA cert) — say “HIPAA-aware with BAA training + audit discipline.”

**UK/EU GDPR + NHS overlays:** Lawful basis (consent/contract/legal obligation/vital interests) + explicit consent for health (Article 9), data minimization, accuracy, storage limit, integrity, accountability + DPIA + DPO; rights (access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection); 72-hour supervisory breach notice + communication if high risk; Data Processing Agreements with processors; NHS Data Security and Protection Toolkit + Caldicott Principles (share minimum, need-to-know, legal basis). Language: “With your consent for appointment coordination... You may request copy/correction/deletion via privacy@... We keep 7 years per policy...”

**Australia Privacy Act + My Health Record:** 13 Australian Privacy Principles (open management, anonymity option where lawful, collection solicitation, unsolicited data, notification, use/disclosure limits, direct marketing opt-out, cross-border, identifiers, quality, security, access, correction). My Health Record sensitive — access controls + patient controls. Emergency numbers 000. Terms: Medicare, GP, allied health. Same habits: minimum, verified, approved, logged.

In interviews: “Kenya DPA-trained, HIPAA-aware via BAA, GDPR-aware for UK clients — encrypted devices, approved channels only, no PHI in public AI unless employer authorizes BAA-covered tool in writing, 1-hour incident reporting, annual refreshers. Here’s my workspace audit + phishing log.” Bring proof, not claims.

## 7. AI and Personal Channels: The Fastest Way to Get Fired (And How to Use AI Safely)

Forbidden: pasting names/DOB/MRN/phones/photos/IDs/diagnoses/Rx into public ChatGPT/Gemini/Copilot/free summarizers/transcribers/grammar checkers that retain data; uploading EHR PDFs to free tools; letting AI email patients directly; asking AI for dosage/triage to tell patient. Free = no BAA = breach even if “just to draft nicely.” One paste = reportable breach + dismissal. Managers check history + DLP alerts.

Allowed only with guardrails: de-identified generic prompts on public AI (“Draft polite appointment reminder template with placeholders, no PHI, HIPAA-aware tone”) → human edit + verify + send via approved channel + document; BAA-covered enterprise AI (EHR DAX, Copilot with BAA in writing) for transcription/summarization with 100% human verification of meds/doses/plan + “AI-assisted draft verified by [Name date]” note + delete local audio per retention. When in doubt: don’t paste, handle manually in EHR, ask supervisor “Is [tool] BAA-covered and authorized for [purpose] in writing?” Document answer.

Refuse unsafe requests professionally: “I can’t send via personal WhatsApp/Gmail or paste into public AI per privacy law and BAA — to protect us both I’ll use verified EHR/portal now (2 min) and confirm receipt. For speed I’ve prepared de-identified draft without patient details for your approval — shall I proceed?” + log request + refusal + secure alternative offered. Speed + safety beats insecure speed.
